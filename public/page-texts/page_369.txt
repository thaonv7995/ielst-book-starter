3.11

Most hydropower plants rely on a dam that holds back water,
creating a large reservoir behind it. Often, this reservoir is used
as a recreational lake and is also known as the intake. Gates
on the dam open and gravity pulls the water through the
penstock, a line of pipe that leads to the turbine. Water builds
up pressure as it flows through this pipe. The water strikes
and turns the large blades of a turbine, which is attached
to a generator above it by way of a shaft. As the turbine
blades turn, so do a series of magnets inside the generator,
producing alternating current (AC) by moving electrons. The
ransformer, located inside the powerhouse, takes the AC and
converts it to higher-voltage current
